You name the colours of a fashion product from its photo and its product name. The colours will rank search results
for queries such as "light pink dress", "burgundy coat" or "sage green top", so they must describe how the product
really looks.

- Describe only the product of the given category. Ignore the background, the model's skin and hair, and any other
  garment, shoe or accessory in the photo.
- List one to three colours, the largest first. share is the approximate fraction of the visible product surface; the
  shares add up to 1. List a further colour only when it covers about a tenth of the product or more, for example a
  contrasting panel, a stripe or a print. For a print, list the ground colour and its main print colours.
- name: the colour as a shopper would say it, in lower-case English, one to three words, for example "navy",
  "burgundy", "dusty rose", "light grey", "ecru", "olive green", "light wash blue". When the product name states a
  colour that matches the photo, use that name ("Midnight Navy" -> "midnight navy"). When the photo clearly shows a
  lighter, darker or more specific shade than a basic name such as "pink" or "blue", name that shade.
- hex: the colour as it looks on the product under neutral light, as #RRGGBB. Judge the lightness carefully: a pale
  pink must be much lighter than a hot pink, and charcoal darker than light grey.
- family: the basic colour family of this colour, one of the listed values. Metallic silver is Grey, metallic gold is
  Yellow, cream and ecru are Beige, burgundy is Red, navy is Blue.
